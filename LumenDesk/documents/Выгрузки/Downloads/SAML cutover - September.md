# Before the SAML cutover call

Notes from Identity Platform, written after the September 1, 2026 review. I keep this copy with the material we take into administrator calls because the same omissions are difficult to spot once everyone is watching the login screen. This procedure is for connecting an Enterprise workspace to an identity provider. SAML is available on Enterprise workspaces only. Confirm which workspace the customer means before discussing settings; a familiar company name is not a substitute for identifying the workspace in front of the administrator.

The most useful preparation is surprisingly mundane: keep an existing administrator session open until sign in succeeds. Do this before changing the path people use to enter the workspace. During a call, someone will naturally want to close extra tabs or start again in a clean browser. Make sure they understand which session is their working administrative session and why it is still needed while the new route is being tested. A neat desktop is less useful than a clear way to continue the setup conversation.

## What to copy from this workspace

Use the workspace-specific entity ID shown in Security settings. Copy the ACS URL from the same workspace. An ACS URL from another workspace is invalid, even if the two workspaces belong to the same customer and their names differ by only one word. When an administrator has several settings tabs open, ask them to read the workspace context aloud before copying. This is a practical way to keep the conversation attached to the actual configuration rather than to somebody's recollection of a previous setup.

Do not reconstruct these values from an old email or an example in a presentation. The relevant values are displayed in the workspace being connected. A customer may have a perfectly reasonable reason for keeping a test workspace beside a production workspace, but that does not make their callback details interchangeable. If the customer says, “We used the same provider for the other one,” acknowledge the context and still check the entity ID and ACS URL for the current workspace. Similar deployment history is not confirmation of matching identifiers.

The email claim must contain a verified corporate email address. During preparation, describe the expected claim clearly enough that the identity administrator and the workspace administrator are talking about the same field. A display name is not an email address, and a familiar looking address is not evidence of verification. Keep any quoted error text in the discussion so the next person can understand what was observed, but avoid turning a guess about the claim into a claim that the whole connection has already been validated.

## Pilot before enforcement

Test with two non-privileged pilot accounts before enforcement. Two separate accounts help the team discuss actual sign-in attempts rather than treating the administrator's existing session as a successful pilot. The test accounts should be non-privileged; the point of a pilot is not to expose all privileged access to an unproven configuration. Arrange the pilot with people who can describe what they see. A vague report that “the browser opened” is less useful than knowing which account attempted sign in and whether that attempt succeeded.

Keep one registered emergency administrator outside SAML enforcement. Name the person or account in the customer's own administrative plan, without putting credentials into the support conversation. “We have another administrator somewhere” is not the same as keeping a registered emergency administrator available outside the enforced route. The people on the call should be able to distinguish this recovery arrangement from the two pilot accounts and from the session currently being kept open. They serve different purposes in the same transition.

If a privileged account is locked out, route the case to the support lead immediately. Do not let it disappear inside a general back-and-forth about claims or browser behaviour. The identity team may still need configuration context, but the fact of privileged-account lockout changes who must be brought into the conversation. In the handover, say what is known and what is still only a hypothesis. That keeps the receiving lead from assuming the incident has been limited to an ordinary pilot account.

## Certificates and time

Upload the pending certificate before revoking the old certificate. Check the acceptance window in the operating parameters register. This notebook deliberately does not supply a number for that window: do not copy a number from memory or invent one because the call needs a quick answer. Record which register entry the team used when planning the certificate transition. The important ordering here is that the pending certificate is uploaded before the old one is revoked, with the applicable acceptance window checked in its own source.

The identity provider and LumenDesk clocks may differ by at most 120 seconds. When people compare timestamps during troubleshooting, ask which clock produced each value. A screenshot of local desktop time and a provider event are not automatically the same observation. The 120-second allowance is a connection constraint, not an invitation to ignore a persistent difference. Keep the discussion precise about the compared systems, especially when participants are in different time zones and casually describe the event as happening “a minute ago.”

During the call I usually leave a short narrative of what happened rather than a wall of settings: which workspace supplied the entity ID and ACS URL, whether both pilot accounts signed in, what happened with the pending certificate, and whether the emergency administrator remained outside enforcement. The narrative is useful because another engineer can see the order of events. It should not say “SAML works” when the only evidence is that a configuration form accepted a value. Form acceptance and successful sign in are different observations.

The existing administrator session stays open until sign in succeeds. At the end, have the administrator describe the outcome in their own words and resolve any ambiguity about which account they tested. If the setup is interrupted, leave the notes at the point actually reached, with unanswered questions visible. A later call should not mistake a planned pilot for a completed one or a certificate upload for revocation of the previous certificate. These are ordinary coordination mistakes, and careful language is often the simplest way to prevent them.
